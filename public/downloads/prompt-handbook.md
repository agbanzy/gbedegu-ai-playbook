## 1. The formula: six parts of a great prompt

Every strong prompt has some mix of these six elements. You don't need all six every time — but when an answer disappoints you, one of these is usually missing.

**Role** — tell the AI who to be.
> "You are a brand strategist who works with African SMEs."

**Context** — the background it can't guess.
> "My audience is young professionals in Abuja. My goal is enquiries, not likes."

**Task** — the verb. What exactly should it produce?
> "Write my Instagram bio and a pinned-post caption."

**Constraints** — length, tone, format, what to avoid.
> "150 words max, warm but expert tone, no hashtags, no jargon."

**Examples** — show 1–3 samples of what "good" looks like.
> "Here are two bios I love — match this energy: …"

**Iterate** — treat answer #1 as draft #1.
> "Make option 2 bolder. Now shorten it. Now end with a question."

## 2. Right vs wrong — the same 30 seconds

**Wrong:**
> "Write a LinkedIn post about my business."

Result: generic, could be anyone's business, you rewrite it anyway.

**Right:**
> "You are a LinkedIn ghostwriter for a Nigerian fintech founder **[ROLE]**. My audience: young professionals in Abuja **[CONTEXT]**. Write a 150-word launch post for our payments app — warm, confident, no hashtags, end with a question **[TASK + CONSTRAINTS]**. Here's a post whose tone I like: … **[EXAMPLE]**"

Result: sounds like you, ready to post, first try.

## 3. Four power moves

1. **Show examples (few-shot).** Paste 2–3 samples of what good looks like. The single fastest quality upgrade — it copies your taste.
2. **Make it plan first.** "Before writing, show me your outline." Approve the plan, then let it write. Fewer rewrites.
3. **Force the format.** "Give me a table / a checklist / 3 options ranked with trade-offs." You get structure you can act on.
4. **Run a critique loop.** "Now critique your draft against my goals, then rewrite it." A built-in editor, free.

## 4. Role prompts to save today

Copy these, fill the brackets, save them in your assistant's Projects (or as a custom GPT / skill):

- "You are my **brand strategist**. My business: [what you do]. My customer: [who]. Audit my [bio / website / pitch] and rewrite it to speak directly to that customer."
- "You are my **sales coach**. Here's a real customer conversation that stalled: [paste]. Diagnose where I lost them and script three ways I could have advanced it."
- "You are my **market researcher**. Compare the top 5 [your industry] providers in [city] on price, positioning, and reviews. Table, then a gap I could own."
- "You are my **CFO**. Here are my numbers: [paste]. Where am I leaking money, and what are my three highest-leverage fixes?"
- "You are my **content editor**. Take this idea: [paste] and turn it into: a LinkedIn post, an Instagram caption, a WhatsApp status, and a 30-second video script — same message, native to each platform."

## 5. From prompts to systems — the leverage ladder

| Rung | What it is | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Prompt** | One ask, one answer | "Draft this email" |
| **Workflow** | Chained steps, repeatable | Research → outline → draft → edit, saved as one recipe |
| **Agent** | A goal + tools + permission | "Find 20 venues, compare prices, draft outreach" — it browses and builds the sheet |
| **Loop** | Draft → critique → improve, repeated | A critic AI scores each draft against your checklist until it passes |
| **Graph** | A team of agents, each owning a step | Researcher → Writer → Fact-checker → Designer → you approve |

Each rung: less of your time per unit of output. Climb one rung this month.

## 6. The guardrails

- **AI drafts, you approve** — always, for anything public, legal, or money-related.
- **Never paste secrets** (passwords, API keys, customers' personal data) into a chat.
- **Verify claims** the AI makes about the real world — ask it for sources, spot-check them.
- **Your voice is the moat.** Edit every draft until it sounds like you; the tools are the same for everyone, taste is not.

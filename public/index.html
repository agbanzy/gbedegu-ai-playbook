1990s

2010s

Work from anywhere. The office stopped being a place.

Right vs wrong — the same thirty seconds

Wrong The prompt most people write

Write a LinkedIn post about my business.
Generic. Could be anyone's business. You'll rewrite it anyway — so you saved nothing.

Right The prompt that works

You are a LinkedIn ghostwriter for a Nigerian fintech founder [ROLE]. My audience: young professionals in Abuja [CONTEXT]. Write a 150-word launch post for our payments app — warm, confident, no hashtags, end with a question [TASK + CONSTRAINTS]. Here's a post whose tone I like: … [EXAMPLE]
Sounds like you. Ready to post — first try.

Four power moves

  1. Show examples (few-shot)Paste two or three samples of what good looks like. The single fastest quality upgrade there is — it copies your taste, and taste is the part it can't guess.
  2. Make it plan first“Before writing, show me your outline.” Approve the plan, then let it write. Far fewer rewrites than correcting a finished draft.
  3. Force the format“Give me a table / a checklist / three options ranked with trade-offs.” You get structure you can act on instead of an essay you have to mine.
  4. Run a critique loop“Now critique your draft against my goals, then rewrite it.” A built-in editor, free, and it catches things you'd have sent.

From prompts to systems — the leverage ladder

Each rung costs you less time per unit of output. Climb exactly one rung this month.

1PromptOne ask, one answer.“Draft this email.”
2WorkflowChained steps, repeatable.Research → outline → draft → edit, saved as one recipe.
3AgentA goal, plus tools, plus permission.“Find 20 venues, compare prices, draft outreach” — it browses and builds the sheet.
4LoopDraft → critique → improve, repeated.A critic AI scores each draft against your checklist until it passes.
5GraphA team of agents, each owning a step.Researcher → Writer → Fact-checker → Designer → you approve.
The guardrails. AI drafts — you approve anything public, legal, or money-related. Never paste secrets or customers' personal data into a chat. Verify the claims it makes about the real world and ask for sources. And edit every draft until it sounds like you: the tools are the same for everyone, taste is not.

Part 3

The AI Coworker Flow

Stop treating AI like Google — ask, get, leave. Start treating it like a capable junior colleague: brief it, give it materials, review its work, and let it keep context between conversations.

1 · Set up its desk (one hour, once)

  1. Pick one assistant and make it a homeClaude, ChatGPT or Gemini — then create a Project or workspace for your business. One tool mastered beats ten tabs open.
  2. Upload what a new hire would needWhat you sell, your prices, your best posts, your tone, your FAQs, a description of your target customer.
  3. Write standing instructions“Always write in my voice — warm, direct, no jargon. My customer is X. Never invent prices — ask me.”
  4. Save your five role prompts inside itNow every conversation starts with a coworker who already knows your business.

2 · The daily flow (15–30 minutes)

Morning brief · 5 min

“Here's what's on today: [three items]. Draft the two emails, outline the proposal, and list what you need from me.”

Midday review · 10 min

Read its drafts like a manager: approve, edit, or send back with one line of feedback — “warmer”, “shorter”, “add the price table”.

Evening close · 5 min

“Summarize what we finished, what's pending, and pre-draft tomorrow's first task.”

3 · The briefing framework

Brief AI the way you'd brief a contractor — six lines, and the quality jumps immediately.

Role

Who it should be for this task.

Goal

What “done” actually looks like.

Materials

Paste or attach everything relevant.

Constraints

Format, length, tone, budget — deadline-level detail.

Exclusions

What NOT to do. “Don't contact anyone, don't invent numbers.”

Checkpoint

“Show me the plan before executing.”

4 · Review gates (non-negotiable)

Work typeYour gate
Internal drafts, research, summariesSkim and go.
Anything public — posts, emails to clientsYou read every word before it sends.
Money, legal, contracts, pricingYou decide. AI only prepares the options.
Customers' personal dataDon't paste it in at all.

5 · Three starter automations

The lead machine

Enquiry from Instagram, WhatsApp or a web form → AI drafts a reply in your voice → the lead is logged to a sheet or CRM → a day-3 follow-up draft → a Monday-morning summary to you.

₦0 – $20/mo Zapier · Make · n8n

The content engine

One long piece a week — a voice note counts — cut by AI into posts, captions, clips and a newsletter, then scheduled through Buffer.

Free tier works Opus Clip · Buffer

The inbox triager

New emails labelled by AI — lead, invoice, spam, urgent — into a daily digest with a suggested one-line reply for each.

₦0 self-hosted n8n · Gmail

Scaling: from coworker to team. Delegate the repetitive to automations — they work 24/7 without a salary. Hire agents like junior staff: research, first drafts, data entry, QA — briefed like interns, reviewed like interns. Systemize, so your winning prompts become SOPs your whole team reuses. The guardrail: humans stay on money, legal, and anything public-facing. Reputation can't be regenerated.

Twenty-one steps to a working AI-powered brand

Nothing ticked yet. Start with Week 1 — picking one assistant takes four minutes.

The three rules that make it stick.
  1. AI drafts — you approve anything public, legal, or money-related.
  2. One paid tool at a time, and only when it passes a test: you hit the free limit weekly, it saves 2+ hours a month, or it touches revenue.
  3. Consistency for 90 days beats brilliance for one week.

If you remember five things

1 · You are still early

Every era rewarded early adapters — the first businesses online, the first brands on social. With AI, that window is open right now.

2 · Prompt like a manager

Role, context, constraints, examples, iterate. Brief it the way you'd brief a person you're paying.

3 · One assistant, mastered

One paid assistant you know deeply beats ten browser tabs of tools you half-use.

4 · Ship something this week

A one-page site, hosted free, with a QR code on your card. Done beats planned.

5 · Your voice is the moat

The tools are the same for everyone. Taste is not. Edit every draft until it sounds like you — that's the part no model can copy from a prompt.
